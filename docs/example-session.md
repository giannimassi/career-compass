# Example Session (Redacted)

A synthetic example of what a first-session conversation might feel like. Not a transcript — illustrative only.

User starts a new ChatGPT conversation, pastes the prompt, then types their opening message.

---

**User:** I got fired from my marketing job three months ago. I'm 34, I've been looking since, but I feel like I've been applying to a lot of different things without much direction. Nothing's worked.

**Advisor:** Job searching after a sudden exit is genuinely hard, especially when the direction piece gets blurry under the pressure of needing income. This conversation can help you step back and figure out where you actually are versus where you're trying to go, and get you one or two things to do this week that will move you.

Before anything else — where are you in your search right now? I'm asking about the emotional read, not just the logistics.

**User:** Honestly, pretty burnt out. I spent the first month sending out tons of applications — like 80 in six weeks — and maybe got two recruiter calls, both cold. The last month I've barely done anything. I open LinkedIn, look around, close it.

**Advisor:** That pattern — heavy early effort, low returns, then the applications dry up — is documented and predictable. It's not character failure, it's a confidence loop. I'll come back to that.

I can work with you in two ways:
- **Light mode**: about 15-20 minutes, we focus on one clear output, no pressure
- **Deep mode**: 60-90 minutes, we go through the whole picture and build a Session Summary at the end that you can bring back next time to pick up where we left off

Which fits today?

**User:** Deep. I have time.

**Advisor:** Good. Before I pick the right starting point, I need to place you in the right stage. One question: have you committed to a specific role type and industry to pursue, or is that part still open?

**User:** It's more open than I want to admit. I was in B2B SaaS marketing but I'm not sure I want to go back to that. I've been applying to marketing roles, comms roles, a couple of customer success roles...

**Advisor:** That tells me something important. You're "applying to lots of things" but you haven't committed to a target. That's Exploring behavior in an Executing shell — which is why the applications aren't converting. The 80 applications didn't fail because of a tactical problem. They failed because they were spread across roles that don't share a story.

I'm placing you at **Orienting**, with a Stuck overlay. Does that fit, or does something feel off?

**User:** Yeah, that fits. I think I was avoiding admitting I don't know what I want.

**Advisor:** Common, and it's the right thing to acknowledge. Here's what I'd propose: first we do **Direction setting** — we find a one-sentence north star. Then we do **Positioning**, producing a 2–3 sentence pitch that makes you compelling for that direction. That should take maybe 45 minutes, and you'll leave with two concrete artifacts. Any reason to adjust?

**User:** No, let's do that.

**Advisor:** Before we narrow things down — are there any deal-breakers I should know about? Things that would make a role wrong for you immediately, regardless of anything else?

**User:** Probably two. I can't go back to a company with no PTO boundaries — my last place was 60-hour weeks and that's what burnt me out. And I need at least 70k, ideally a bit more.

**Advisor:** Good. Those are both real signals. Compensation floor of 70k, working-hours boundary non-negotiable. I'll keep them anchored.

Now, role type. You mentioned marketing, comms, and customer success. Which of those feels most like "I could see myself doing this for three more years"?

*(conversation continues through direction setting, then positioning, each producing a concrete artifact...)*

## What this illustrates

- **Stage-first, not tactics-first**: the advisor didn't launch into "let me help you improve your CV" — it first figured out that the user was Orienting mislabeled as Executing, which changes everything downstream.
- **Stuck overlay detection**: the pattern of "80 apps → 2 responses → pullback" was recognized and reframed as a confidence loop, not character failure.
- **Artifacts, not just advice**: the session produced three concrete things (values, north star, pitch) the user can use.
- **Silent profile inference**: you can see in the INTERNAL PROFILE block that the advisor built a read of the user's style without ever naming it in conversation.
- **Multi-session continuity**: the Session Summary makes the next session resumable without re-explaining context.
